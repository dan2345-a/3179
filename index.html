Tourists on the move

About 45% of international visitors now come on holiday. Numbers fell to almost zero when the border closed, then came back after it reopened in February 2022. Countries like Malaysia has long been one of the top sources of visitors each year.

Where nature is protected

Australia's wildlife is best seen in its national parks and reserves. How much land each state protects varies a lot, and so does the size of its parks: some cover a few hectares, others are bigger than some countries.

Good to know

Indigenous Protected Areas are managed by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities. Many welcome visitors, but some need a permit first.

National parksare the easiet places to visit. Most have marked walking tracks, visitor center and campgrounds

Where to find them

Five of our eight animals live in the wild only in Australia. Each has its own home range. Pick one to see where people spotted it in the last five years.

Koalas live only in eastern and south-eastern Australia, among the eucalyptus forests of Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and South Australia.

Quokkas are mostly found on Rottnest Island, a short ferry ride from Perth.

Tasmanian devils now live in the wild only on the island of Tasmania.

Saltwater crocodiles also live in Malaysia, in Sarawak's rivers. In Australia they live in the tropical north. River cruises in Kakadu are a safe way to see them.

Little penguins nest along the southern coast. Several colonies can be watched as they come ashore at dusk.

Quokkas are the most packed together, as 63% of the sightings are from Perth and Rottnest Island.

Saltwater crocodiles are mostly around Darwin, with half of all sightings in one square.

Little penguins mostly on Kangaroo Island and Tasmanian Devils around Cradle Mountain

Koalas and kangaroos are spread around along the east coast and their top squares are just south and west of Sydney.

Platypuses are the most spread out, as they look for quiet creeks.

When to go

Australia's seasons are the opposite of the northern hemisphere's. Many animals are easier to see at certain times of year. Does the best time to see them match the busiest tourist season?

What the data says

Spring (October-December)is the peak for koalas, little penguins and Tasmanian devils. It overlaps the busiest tourist months.

Winter (June-September)is best for whales and saltwater crocodiles, and it is quieter for visitors. Fewer crowds, more wildlife.

Follow the whales

Every year, tens of thousands of humpback whales swim north from Antarctica to breed in warm waters, then return south with their calves. Both coasts become some of the best whale-watching spots in the world.

Heading north (around May-August). Whales travel up the east and west coasts towards the warm waters of Queensland and the Kimberley.

Heading south (around September-November). Mothers and calves swim slowly back, often resting in sheltered bays such as Hervey Bay.

Best months: August and September. Each has about 27% of the whole year's whale sightings.

Travel tip: whale season (June-October) falls in the quieter months for international visitors. Compare it with the blue bands in section 4.

More eyes in the wild

Every sighting on this page was recorded by a person: a ranger, a scientist or a visitor with a phone. For the rarest animals, most records now come from the public.

Your turn

More records do not always mean more animals. They often mean more people are watching and sharing.

When you visit, you can add to this data too. Photograph wildlife from a safe distance and upload it to iNaturalist or the Atlas of Living Australia.

Keep your distance, never feed wild animals, and follow park rules.

Putting it together

Back to our three questions: where to go, when to go, and what you are likely to see.

Where. Each animal has its own hotspot, in a different part of the country: koalas in New South Wales, crocodiles around Darwin, quokkas in Western Australia and devils in Tasmania. Plan around two or three regions, not the whole map. Only 22.6% of the land is protected, so national parks are the best places to start.

When. Spring (October-November) is the best season for koalas, kangaroos, little penguins and Tasmanian devils. It overlaps with the busy holiday months. Winter is quieter and is the time for whales and saltwater crocodiles.

What. Five of the eight animals live in the wild only in Australia. The rarer the animal, the more its sightings come from visitors with phones. That is why your own photos matter.
